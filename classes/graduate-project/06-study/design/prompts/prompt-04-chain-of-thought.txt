Level 4 is the level 3 prompt, unchanged, followed by one paragraph asking the
AI to reason through the requirements step by step before answering. Nothing
else differs from level 3.

Copy-and-paste file: chain-of-thought-copy-and-paste.txt (this folder)
Attachments: the same two files as levels 1 to 3 (one diagram, unchanged; the
verbatim specification).

The added paragraph (sent to the AI exactly as written):

    Before you give me the table, work through it step by step. Take the
    requirements one at a time, in order. For each one, find where the diagram
    shows that information, say what you found, and then decide if it meets.
    When you've gone through all of them, give me the table.

How the paragraph was written
-----------------------------
The technique is zero-shot chain-of-thought: an instruction to reason step by
step before answering, with no worked reasoning examples. Kojima et al. (2022)
showed that "LLMs are decent zero-shot reasoners by simply adding 'Let's think
step by step' before each answer" (p. 1), and that the method "does not need
any examples" (p. 2). The core idea is to "add Let's think step by step, or a
similar text (see Table 4), to extract step-by-step reasoning" (p. 3).

Table 4 (p. 8) compares sixteen wordings of the instruction. Among them is
"Let's solve this problem by splitting it into steps" (72.2%), alongside
"Let's think step by step" (78.7%). The paragraph used here is of that kind:
it keeps "step by step" and states, in the words a worker would use, how the
task splits into steps: take the requirements one at a time, in order; find
the information on the diagram; say what was found; then decide. Reasoning is
placed before the answer ("Before you give me the table ... When you've gone
through all of them, give me the table"), as the technique requires.

The level 2 request "Keep it short" is left unchanged. A worker may ask for
both a careful step-by-step check and a short answer; how the AI handles the
two is part of what level 4 shows.

Source PDF, with these passages highlighted:
../../../04-literature/papers/Kojima, 2022, Large Language Models are Zero-Shot
Reasoners, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems.pdf
