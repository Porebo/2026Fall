Page Content Owners and Operators of Underground Injection Control (UIC) wells and facilities in California are required to comply with applicable laws and regulations to obtain an approval to operate injection wells.  
Final Text of the UIC Regulations (PDF) On March 17, 2026, the California Department of Conservation's Geologic Energy Management Division (CalGEM) hosted an informational workshop for UIC operators regarding implementation updates to the Class II UIC project review and approval process. This updated process applies to all UIC project types including proposed new projects, existing projects subject to CalGEM “Project-by-Project" (PbP) reviews, and proposed expansions of existing UIC projects, but not proposed non-expansion projects.  The accompanying UIC Workshop Presentation, provided below, highlights those requirements and the updated project review and approval processes.   UIC Workshop Presentation Slides (PDF) 
UIC Project Inter-Agency Coordinat ion The Revised Memorandum of Agreement ("MOA'') between the State Water Resources Control Board (Board or State Water Board) and CalGEM regarding Underground Injection Control, Discharges to Land, and Other Issues (Revised MOA) documents how the agencies coordinate the administration of the State's UIC program for Class II wells, regulating discharges to land of produced water from oil and gas operations, responding to incidents such as spills, taking enforcement actions and handling other related issues. The procedures in the Revised MOA are intended to provide a coordinated approach resulting in a single approval satisfying the statutory obligations of both agencies in regulating the injection of fluids into Class II wells and a single approval in regulating the discharge of produced water from oil and gas operations to land. *NEW*  The Revised MOA with the State Water Board has been updated with new appendices (see further below) effective April 1, 2026.  Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) – 2018 (PDF)Revised Ap pendix and Atta chments - March 2026 (PDF) MOA Checklist One of the Revised MOA new appendices includes the updated MOA checklist or “Project Information Checklist.” This guidance document is used by CalGEM and the State Water Board to coordinate inter-agency functions related to regulation of Class II underground injection operations. UIC Pre-Applicat ion Me eting: 
CalGEM’s updated UIC project review and approval process expects operators to schedule a “Pre-Application” meeting prior to application submittal. Operators can request this early meeting by submitting the UIC Pre-Application Meeting Form (see below), along with any questions for discussion. CalGEM staff will then coordinate with the State Water Board and the operator to schedule the meeting. This early conversation is intended to help identify potential issues and provide helpful guidance before a formal UIC application is submitted through the Well Statewide Tracking and Reporting System (WellSTAR) - UIC Application for Injection Approval form.

Application for Injection Approval  
An operator requesting approval for an underground injection project must provide detailed data that, in CalGEM’s judgment, are pertinent and necessary for the evaluation of a proposed project. (14 CCR § 1724.7.) The application must include a detailed engineering study that includes a statement of the primary purpose of the project; the reservoir and fluid characteristics of each injection zone; and the planned well drilling and plugging and abandonment program to complete the project, including a flood-pattern map showing all injection, production, and plugged and abandoned wells, and unit boundaries. The engineering study must also include casing diagrams for all idle, plugged and abandoned, and deeper zone producing wells within the area affected by the project. The casing diagrams in the engineering study must evidence that plugged and abandoned wells in the area will not have an adverse effect on the project or cause damage to life, health, property, or natural resources. (14 CCR § 1724.7(a).) 
Along with the engineering study, a geologic study and injection plan must also be submitted. At a minimum, the geologic study must include a structural and isopach map, at least two cross sections in the area of review through at least three wells including one injection well, and a representative electric log that identifies all geologic units, formations, freshwater aquifers, and oil or gas zones. (14 CCR § 1724.7(a)(2).) The injection plan must include a map showing all injection facilities; maximum anticipated injection pressure and volumes; monitoring system or method used to ensure that injection fluid is confined to the intended zone or zones of injection; method of injection; corrosion protective measures; the source, analysis, and treatment of the injection fluid; and the location and depth of water-source wells to be used in conjunction with the project. (14 CCR § 1724.7(a)(3).)  
Additional information can be requested for projects that may be hazardous, large, unusual, or particularly complex. (14 CCR § 1724.7(e).) In instances where an operator desires to change or modify any of the originally approved operating methods or conditions of a project, such as expansion of the project, change of the injection interval, or an increase in the maximum allowable surface injection pressure, the operator must first obtain approval from CalGEM. (14 CCR § 1724.10(a).)  
In order to expedite the application process, operators should evaluate the data being submitted to determine if the program requirements discussed below will be met. It is the operator’s responsibility to demonstrate to CalGEM’s satisfaction that the program requirements are met.   
Area of Review
As explained above, every injection project must be supported by an engineering study that includes casing diagrams for all idle, plugged and abandoned, and deeper zone producing wells within the area affected by the project. The casing diagrams in the engineering study must evidence that plugged and abandoned wells in the area will not have an adverse effect on the project or cause damage to life, health, property, or natural resources. (14 CCR § 1724.7(a).) Additionally, CalGEM must evaluate casing diagrams for all wells within the area affected by the project including directionally drilled wells that intersect the injection zone or a deeper zone within the area affected. 
Boundary Area of Review
In determining the “area affected by the project”, CalGEM’s definition of the Area of Review (AOR) is consistent with the federal Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA) regulations. (14 CCR § 1720.1(a).) Under the federal regulations, the AOR is a minimum fixed radius of one-quarter-mile from the well bore, unless an approved mathematical model is used to determine the “zone of endangering influence.” (40 CFR § 146.6.) The federal regulation provides a modified “This equation” as an example of such a mathematical model. (40 CFR § 146.6(a)(2).) CalGEM’s regulations do not refer to the “zone of endangering influence” but rather describe this as the calculated distance that injected fluid may migrate. For an AOR using a fixed one-quarter-mile radius the following factors are to be taken into consideration: chemistry of the injected and formation fluids; hydrogeology; population; ground water use and dependence; and historical injection practices in the area. (40 CFR § 146.6(b).)   
CalGEM nominally defines an AOR boundary for a proposed injection well as a fixed one-quarter-mile perimeter around the portion of the well’s path that lies within the approved zone of injection as projected on a horizontal plane at ground level. However, this definition is a starting point in determining an AOR boundary. The AOR boundary may be reduced or expanded based on pertinent information regarding the well’s completion, the local geology, and the injection parameters, including approved mathematical modeling.  
Confinement to the Intended Zone  
Every injection project must include an injection plan that ensures that the injection fluid will be confined to the intended zone or zones of injection. (14 CCR § 1724.7(e)(3).) To confirm that injection fluid is confined to the approved zone or zones, mechanical integrity testing must be performed on each injection well upon commencement of injection and periodically thereafter. (14 CCR § 1724.10(i).)  
In order to determine whether abandoned wells within the AOR could act as a conduit for injection fluid, well data must be examined to ascertain the condition of the wells. Consistent with the requirements discussed below, it is safe to assume that a well will not act as a conduit if it has a verified 100 linear feet of cement above the approved zone of injection, or a calculated 150 linear feet of cement above the approved zone of injection. (When the top of cement is determined by calculation a substantial margin of safety is needed to allow for variations in hole size, cementing procedures, pre-flush conditions and displacement of fluids.) For abandoned wells, consideration must be given to the cement both inside and outside all casing strings, but for active and idle wells, only the cement outside of all casing strings is relevant.  
The specific requirements to be considered when examining the data for wells within the AOR include the following:  
Plugged and abandoned wells are required to have oil-bearing or gas-bearing strata isolated. Operators are required to use every effort and endeavor to protect any underground or surface water suitable for irrigation or domestic purposes from the infiltration of detrimental substances. (Pub. Resources Code, § 3228.) State Water Resources Control Board Resolution No. 88-63 (Appendix A) states that water with total dissolved solids (TDS) of 3,000 mg/L or less is suitable, or potentially suitable, for municipal or domestic use. An aquifer exemption may be required for water with TDS between 3,000 and 10,000 mg/L. (40 CFR 146.4.)  
Current regulation requires operators to fill the annular space to at least 500 feet above oil and gas zones and anomalous pressure intervals. However, prior to 1978, operators were only required to have 100 feet of cement in the annular space above oil and gas zones and anomalous pressure intervals. CalGEM may provide a variance to the current requirement if supported by known geological conditions. (14 CCR § 1722.4.)  
Plugged and abandoned wells are required to have cement across all perforations and the plug shall extend at least 100 feet above the top of a landed liner, the uppermost perforations, the casing cementing point, the water shutoff holes, or the oil and gas zone, whichever is highest. (14 CCR § 1723.1(b).)  
In an open hole, a minimum 200-foot plug is required across all fresh-saltwater interfaces. An interface plug may be placed wholly within a thick shale if the shale separates the freshwater from the brackish or saltwater sands. If there is cement behind the casing across the fresh-saltwater interface, then a 100-foot plug is required inside the casing across the interface. Squeeze-cementing, a cavity shot, or other special plugging procedures may be required in certain circumstances. (14 CCR § 1723.2.)   
Submission of Area of Review Data  
CalGEM recommends that operators provide the information and data detailed below when submitting AOR data in support of active or proposed injection operations. This information and data will generally facilitate CalGEM’s review and expedite a determination of whether it has been satisfactorily demonstrated that wells within the AOR will not have an adverse impact on the injection project or cause damage to life, health, property or natural resources.  
It is CalGEM’s preference to receive all data in electronic format. If spreadsheets are provided electronically, only Microsoft Excel format can be accessed by CalGEM.

Wells in need of remediation. CalGEM encourages operators to lay out the plan for remediating problem wells within the AOR.   
Ong oing Requ irements: 
Periodic Project Review:
Operators are required to maintain data establishing the performance and safety of an injection project and make that data available for periodic review by CalGEM. (14 CCR § 1724.6(d).) It is CalGEM’s policy to review the data for each project at least once every three years. The purposes of the periodic review include:  
Determining if the injection project is still consistent with the terms and conditions of the Project Approval Letter (PAL) and is meeting its purpose.  
Ensuring that all required testing has been performed.  
Determining if there have been any changes to the project, including if any wells within the AOR have been drilled, reworked, or abandoned, and determining that well work was completed appropriately.  
Confirming that the injection fluid is confined to the permitted zone of injection.  
Confirming that no damage to life, health, and natural resources is occurring as a result of the injection project.  
It is the responsibility of both the operator and CalGEM to ensure the injection project is operating as permitted and to ensure that no damage is occurring as a result of the injection.   
Injection Pressure
The maximum allowable surface injection pressure (MASIP) for an injection project must be below the fracture pressure, and prior to sustained injection, a step-rate test must be conducted to determine the MASIP. (14 CCR § 1724.10(h)) CalGEM must be notified in advance of the step-rate test so that it may be witnessed. Test pressure must be from hydrostatic to the pressure required to fracture the injection zone or the proposed injection pressure, whichever occurs first. Friction loss is not used in determining the MASIP, but rather provides for an additional margin of error for safety.  
Operators must monitor all injection wells to confirm that injection is at or below the approved MASIP. If the injection pressure is above the approved MASIP, the operator must immediately reduce the injection pressure.  
Mechanical integrity testing must be performed for each injection well prior to commencement of injection and thereafter per the testing frequency stated in the applicable PAL or CalGEM's regulations. (14 CCR § 1724.10(i).) In addition, all injection piping, valves, and facilities must meet or exceed design standards for the maximum anticipated injection pressure and shall be maintained in a safe and leak-free condition (14 CCR § 1724.10(f)). Where there is only a single string of casing across water protected by the SDWA (10,000 mg/l TDS or less), the SAPT must be tested at the approved MASIP for the well. All tests shall be evaluated to ensure casing integrity, i.e. that there are no leaks in the casing and that the fluid is confined to the permitted zone.  
Injection Well Requirements
CalGEM’s well construction standards, consisting of the use of casing, mud, and cement, serve to prevent fluid migration and the comingling of lesser quality fluids. Injection wells must have, in addition to cement above the oil and gas zones, cement across the base of freshwater interface (BFW) with at least 100 feet above the BFW interface (CCR §1722.4). The hole and casing annulus space, between the top of the cement isolating the oil and gas zones and the base of the cement covering the BFW interface should have heavy mud to prevent the movement of fluids. (14 CCR §§ 1722.6 and 1723(b))  
For water disposal wells with proposed injection into a non-hydrocarbon zone, well construction should be consistent with disposal well completed into depleted oil and gas zones. In order to ensure that injected fluids are confined to the intended zone, there must be 100 feet of cement across and above the top of the intended injection zone.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)
Q: Does the Area of Review (AOR) get reviewed an d confirmed before the wellbore diagram evaluation begins?  Without an approved AOR, how does CalGEM ensure the correct set of wellbores is being evaluated? 
A:  CalGEM reviews the Area of Review (AOR) and Zone of Endangering Influence (ZEI) characterization during its technical review, i.e., after application submission. However, the operator’s AOR and ZEI methodology can be discussed during the pre-application meeting. In addition, CalGEM has moved the review of Wellbore Diagrams to the beginning of the technical review process to provide operators with feedback as early as possible and maximize the time available to make corrections or submit additional documentation. As a result, the wellbores identified in the submitted AOR will generally represent the appropriate wells for evaluation, or any necessary additions can be incorporated early in the review process. 
Q: How do you initiate the pre-application meeting? Do operators need to contact each agency separately? 
A: Operators can request a meeting through CalGEM’s website or by using the following link: 
Pre-Application Meeting Request Form (Micros oft Form). CalGEM UIC staff will then coordinate with the State Water Board, CalGEM CEQA Program, and the operator to schedule a meeting. 
Q: Is a “Pre-Application" meeting required for only new and expansion projects? Specifically, are Project-by-Project reviews and Project Data Updates subject to the "pre-application" meeting requirement? 
A: A Pre-Application meeting is welcome for all project types and modifications including New, Expansion, Non-Expansion (Infill), and Project-by-Project Reviews. An operator is not required to participate in the Pre-Application meeting, but these meetings are intended to act as a forum for operators to get tailored guidance for their proposal so that they can most efficiently navigate the regulatory review process. 
Q: Regarding the separate agency reviews, are there going to be specific documents reviewed by each agency? If agencies are reviewing documents in parallel, how are you going to prevent duplicative or contradictory questions and comments from the two agencies? How do we know who is going to be looking at what documents? 
A: The review process largely follows a similar procedure as before, in that the CalGEM District receives the application (or initiates Project-by-Project review), performs the intake review and technical review stages, then passes the material along to CalGEM’s HQ UIC Program and appropriate staff at the State Water Board and Regional Water Quality Control Board (collectively, the Water Boards). However, the procedure has been updated to allow the Water Boards the opportunity to evaluate the Water Supply wells and surrounding beneficial use water, and provide feedback directly to the operator, in parallel with the CalGEM District’s technical review of the entire application. This is because the Water Boards are largely focused on this specific aspect of the project, have long been responsible for conducting the Water Supply well study and beneficial use water analysis, and the agencies determined that this evaluation need not wait for CalGEM’s technical review to conclude. CalGEM, in turn, will be kept in the loop on any correspondence between the Water Boards and operator. Together, this should limit any potential contradictory questions and comments from the two agencies and avoid duplication of efforts.

Q: If a Project-by-Project review does not have any proposed drilling or additional facilities, does it go through the same CEQA and Water Board reviews or does it qualify for some exemptions? 
A: In issuing a revised Project Approval Letter (PAL) that does not modify the existing UIC project or require remedial work, there may not be any anticipated physical impacts to the environment because no physical work is required. Accordingly, CalGEM, in its role as CEQA Lead Agency, may determine that there is no CEQA project. If this determination is made, no further environmental review would be required.
Please note that this does not affect the Water Board review process, and the project must still be reviewed by the Water Boards.
Q: Is there a regulatory and/or published definition of ‘non-expansion’ and ‘expansion’? 
A: There are no regulatory or published definitions for ‘non-expansion’ and ‘expansion’.
A non-expansion UIC application refers to the addition of a well to an existing UIC project (e.g., an infill well) or any modification to an existing UIC project, including but not limited to well conversion, rework, deepening, redrill, or replacement of a well, provided that the proposed activity is consistent with the terms and conditions of the applicable Project Approval Letter (PAL), does not require amendment or modification of the PAL, and does not enlarge or otherwise alter the approved Area of Review (AOR) for the project.
An expansion UIC application refers to any proposed modification to an existing UIC project that is not consistent with the terms and conditions of the existing Project Approval Letter (PAL), requires an addendum or revision of the PAL, and/or results in the enlargement or alteration of the project’s AOR.  Examples may include, but are not limited to, expansion of the AOR, increases in injection volume, extension of project duration, addition of wells, or authorization of additional injection zones.
CalGEM’s CEQA Program considers the term expansion more broadly, focusing on increases in the capacity, size, use, or operation of a facility, to determine whether any CEQA exemptions apply or if further environmental review is required. Whereas the UIC Program focuses on changes affecting the Area of Review (AOR).
Q: Regardless of an aquifer exemption, are we still being asked to provide the base of freshwater marker picks in an exempted zone? 
A: Yes. CalGEM’s UIC regulations require casing diagrams to identify the base of freshwater. (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 14, § 1724.7.1.) It should be noted that any subsurface water with less than 10,000 mg/L Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) and not exempted is protected under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act. This includes all freshwater. As such, underground sources of drinking water (USDW) picks are much more critical for the evaluation of a given project. 
Q: When preparin g documents, is there a preference for receiving one large document as opposed to multiple smaller documents for each section? 
A: A collated PDF is preferred, with diagrams as a separate document. Additionally, if separate higher resolution maps are required to view labels and symbols clearly, those can also be submitted separately due to file size constraints.   
Q: Clearly posting information and data values on maps is not always a reasonable ask for collated PDF format. 
A: In these cases, a separate, higher resolution map is requested. 
Q: If we have submitted a Project-by-Project application, do we need to use the updated checklist? 
A: Applications submitted before April 1st, 2026, implementation date will likely be able to proceed using the previous edition of the checklist. CalGEM may return applications that are early in their review (i.e., in the intake review phase) based on the quality and completeness of the submitted application. If returned, operators should review and resubmit the application using the updated Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) checklist and the CEQA checklist. In these instances, CalGEM may document the details of its review (conducted before returning the application) on the updated checklist for the benefit of the operator and Water Boards staff, rather than using the previous checklist.
Q: Is it okay for an operator to cross reference the updated checklist when submitting a non-expansion application? 
A: Yes. The updated Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) checklist has more detail that can support a non-expansion request.
Q: A formal and consistent definition of “Modified Project ” would be greatly appreciated. 
A: Modified Project is an internal term to WellSTAR and implies any change to the project.
Q: Are isopach maps permitted? 
A: CalGEM’s UIC regulations say “isopach” (dip-corrected values) maps are required, but historically isochore maps have been provided, despite being labeled as isopach. (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 14, § 1724.7.) For shallow dipping beds, isochore are acceptable but an isopach map may be requested.  
Q: SB 1137 pertains to oil and gas well setback requirements and does not reference or govern Underground Injection Control (UIC) projects. The law never mentions UIC. Why is CalGEM asking about SB 1137 considerations for UIC applications? 
A: In an effort to streamline reviews and efficiently utilize available resources, CalGEM is requesting early and proactive communication during the pre-application meetings. This includes considering the viability of the proposed UIC application if it will require submission of Notices of Intention (NOIs) or new production facilities within a Health Protection Zone (HPZ) (as defined in California Code of Regulations, title 14 (CCR), section 1765.1, subdivision (b)) and will be impacted by the requirements established by and pursuant to Senate Bill (SB) 1137 (Gonzalez, Chapter 365, Statutes of 2022). This early confirmation of the viability of the project ensures State resources will not be used to consider UIC applications that are unlikely to be implementable due to SB 1137 restrictions.
