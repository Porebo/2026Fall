# CalGEM casing diagrams: origin, operator workload and notice

Research notes for the graduate project "AI Review of Casing Diagrams" (MS Information Science).
Researched on November 15, 2026 on the CalGEM website (conservation.ca.gov), using a visible Chrome window driven through Playwright. All web sources were last accessed that day.

## How to read these notes

- Keys such as 2019b refer to the saved PDFs listed in section 5.1. "p." means the PDF page. "int. p." means the page number printed on the document. In the two SRIA files, PDF page = printed page + 6.
- "Inference" marks my own reading or arithmetic. Everything else is stated in the source.

## 1. Short answers

### Q1. When and why did CalGEM start requesting casing diagrams from operators?

Casing diagrams were not first requested in 2015. They were already "an existing data requirement for injection projects" (2019b p.19), and by June 2011 District 3 reported to a US EPA audit that it required operators to submit them (section 2.5). What changed between 2015 and 2019 was how the Division collected and defined them:

1. **October 7, 2015.** The Division notified all operators that it would review every existing UIC project and collect "more detailed data on a project-specific basis that goes beyond the existing requirements" (2018b p.14). This is the closest match to the start of systematic requests. The notice itself was not obtained, and the passage does not name casing diagrams (section 2.3).
2. **October 1, 2018 (gas storage) and April 1, 2019 (injection projects and idle wells).** Standardized minimum content for casing diagrams became mandatory: 14 CCR § 1726.4.1, § 1724.7.1 and § 1772.1.3.

**Why.** The Division uses casing diagrams to check that injection fluid stays in the approved injection zone. Other wells in the area of review that penetrate the zone "could potentially serve as conduits for fluid migration" and must be evaluated, and "Casing diagrams are needed to facilitate this review" (2019b p.19). The diagrams operators had been submitting were of "inconsistent quality and completeness" (2019b p.20) and "do not identify all potential issues with the wells" (2019b p.19), which left the Division with "ongoing concerns about wells within the area of review". Pressures behind the change were the 2011 US EPA audit of the UIC program and the 2011 death of an oilfield worker when the ground gave way beneath him (2019b p.3), the Aliso Canyon gas leak of October 23, 2015 for gas storage (2017 p.1), and, for idle wells, AB 2729 (signed September 9, 2016), which requires the Division to update its idle well regulations (2018a pp.10 and 11).

**Legal basis.** No new bill created the 15-item injection checklist. The Division adopted it under existing Public Resources Code §§ 3013 and 3106 (section 2.4).

**Source document saved for Q1:** the UIC Final Statement of Reasons (2019b).

**Confidence.** High for the 2018 and 2019 dates and the stated reasons. Medium for the October 2015 notice as the start of systematic requests. High that casing diagrams were required before 2008: DOGGR's January 2011 compilation prints the § 1724.7(a)(4) casing diagram text, with no change to the section after the September 3, 1996 filing (2011 pp.38 and 110). Whether the requirement dates to 1978 or 1984 is unverified.

### Q2. How many casing diagrams does an administrative action request?

- **Statewide (inference from the 2018 UIC cost analysis, 2018b):** about 10,142 existing injection wells (27% of 37,563) lacked casing diagrams. The analysis assumes half are produced in each of the first two years (about 5,071 a year), then about 716 a year for new wells. That is **13,006 diagrams over five years**.
- **Concentration:** 12 operators own 94% of the injection wells. The 10 operators with more than 500 injectors own 92.4%, which implies about 9,400 diagrams, an average of about 940 each over two years (illustration). The 102 operators with 10 or fewer injectors would share about 65 diagrams in total.
- **One enforcement action:** Order 1517 (April 24, 2025) concerns 2 UIC projects in the Inglewood field. The complete 31-page packet confirms CalGEM requested all wellbore diagrams for both projects, but gives no total well or diagram count. It does document the workload: by December 16, 2024, the operator reported editing about 52% of East Project diagrams and had not started West Project diagrams.
- **Idle wells:** 8,247 wells idle 15 years or more as of 2018 need an engineering analysis that includes casing diagram information, over a four-year period (about 2,062 a year, inference).
- **Review cycle:** the Division must review each existing injection project at least every three years (§ 1724.6(d)). With 765 active projects that is at least 255 project reviews a year (inference).

**Confidence.** The statewide figures are derived estimates built on 2017 data. The Order 1517 packet does not state an exact well or diagram count.

### Q3. How much notice do operators get?

- **Notice of the rules was long.** About 3.5 years from the October 7, 2015 notice to the April 1, 2019 operative date for injection projects. About 16.4 months from the formal proposal (May 19, 2017) to the October 1, 2018 operative date for gas storage. I found no dated phase-in deadline for casing diagrams in the injection rules (section 4.2).
- **The final text came late.** The injection rules took effect 54 days after filing and the gas storage rules 95 days after filing. An operator that waited for the final text had about two months (injection) or about three months (gas storage).
- **Time pressure sits in individual requests.** Order 1517's October 1, 2024 request gave 17 days (extended to December 20, 80 days after the request). On December 18, the operator requested a further 12 weeks. It reported that by December 16 it had onboarded 6 full-time and 3 part-time contractors and reassigned 3 employees, but had edited only about 52% of the East Project diagrams and had not started on West. An order gives 30 days. The December 2025 gas storage notice sets 60 days after well work, effective immediately.
- **Assessment (inference):** operators had plenty of notice that casing diagram rules were coming, but no scheduled grace period for producing the diagrams. Order 1517 provides direct evidence that a specific two-project request created significant work under short deadlines, even after the operator added staff. The packet does not quantify the total number of diagrams, so it cannot establish a precise workload for this action.

**Confidence.** Dates are exact. The "no dated phase-in" finding rests on the final regulation text and the FSOR. The email-only notices (section 5.2) were not read.

## 2. Q1 detail: when and why

### 2.1 Source document

The UIC Final Statement of Reasons (2019b) explains why new § 1724.7.1 standardizes casing diagrams. On PDF p.19 the Division first says why the diagrams matter: "Ensuring that injection fluid will be confined to the approved injection zone is a key performance standard by which the Division evaluates injection projects. Other wells within the area of review that penetrate the injection zone could potentially serve as conduits for fluid migration, and must therefore be evaluated for integrity and other conditions." It then continues:

> "Casing diagrams are needed to facilitate this review. Although casing diagrams are an existing data requirement for injection projects, the Division's existing regulations do not specifically identify much of the information that the Division finds necessary to properly evaluate the wells within the area of review. As a result, the casing diagrams historically submitted in connection with many existing injection projects do not identify all potential issues with the wells. The Division therefore has ongoing concerns about wells within the area of review for many injection projects. Section 1724.7.1 will address this problem by standardizing the minimum requirements for casing diagrams."

(On the page the continuation spans three paragraphs, and a footnote marker after "projects" is omitted.) Footnote 5 on the same page cites the Division's October 2015 report, "Underground Injection Control Program Report on Permitting and Program Assessment: Reporting Period of Calendar Years 2011-2014" (pp.12, 14 and 16 of that report), for casing diagram deficiencies "as a recurring data gap in the Division's project files for existing injection projects".

On p.20 the Division explains why the content is listed in regulation: "Listing this information in regulation is necessary because the current regulations have resulted in casing diagrams of inconsistent quality and completeness."

The UIC cost analysis (2018b p.14) describes it:

The passage above does not name casing diagrams, so tying this notice to casing diagrams is an inference, supported by the Division's statement that casing diagram gaps were a recurring problem in its project files (2019b p.19, footnote 5).

### 2.4 Why the Division standardized casing diagrams

- **Legal basis (no new bill).** The 2019 injection casing diagram standard was a regulatory update under existing law, not the product of a new statute. § 1724.7.1 cites as authority "Sections 3013 and 3106, Public Resources Code" ([Westlaw note for § 1724.7.1](<Casing Diagrams, 2026, Cal. Code Regs. tit. 14, 1724.7.1, Westlaw.txt>); same note in 2019c). The FSOR frames the whole update as necessary "to carry out its statutory mandate under Public Resources Code section 3106: to prevent, as far as possible, damage to life, health, property, and natural resources" (2019b p.5), and it names no bill (a text search of all 52 pages found no SB or AB reference). The sequence is: existing authority, then identified safety and oversight weaknesses (below), then more specific rules in 2019. By contrast, the gas storage and idle well rules followed new statutes (SB 887 and AB 2729).
- **Quality.** Existing diagrams were "of inconsistent quality and completeness" (2019b p.20) and "do not identify all potential issues with the wells" (2019b p.19).
- **Area of review analyses.** The rulemaking lists "inadequate data (such as casing diagrams) to support area of review analyses" among the problems it addresses (2019b p.4).
- **Federal commitments.** The 1982 primacy agreement commits the Division to evaluate wells within an area of review before approving injection projects (2019b p.3).
- **The 2011 audit and accident.** The FSOR says: "Partially a result of the US EPA audit and the tragic oilfield death, the Division re-examined its UIC program" (2019b p.3). The audit found the Division lacking in "consistent area of review analyses", among other things.
- **Staff effort.** The Division gives the data, not the drawing, as the priority: it "anticipates being able to generate casing diagrams using its own resources so long as the operator provides the data" (2019b p.13). That is why operators may submit a flat file instead of a graphical diagram.
- **Gas storage.** The Aliso Canyon leak (October 23, 2015), the February 2016 emergency regulations and SB 887 (effective January 1, 2017) led to the permanent gas storage regulations (2017 pp.1 and 2).
- **Idle wells.** AB 2729 (signed September 9, 2016) "requires the Division to review, evaluate, and update as appropriate, its regulations pertaining to idle wells" (2018a p.11). The resulting § 1772.1.3 requires casing diagram information in the idle well engineering analysis (2019a p.14).

### 2.5 What operators were already asked before 2015

- **Regulation.** The former § 1724.7(a)(4) required "casing diagrams of wells within the area affected by the project". It was renumbered § 1724.7(a)(1)(C)(iii) in 2019 (2019b p.13). The full pre-2019 wording, as printed in DOGGR's January 2011 compilation (2011 p.38), was: "(4) Casing diagrams, including cement plugs, and actual or calculated cement fill behind casing, of all idle, plugged and abandoned, or deeper-zone producing wells within the area affected by the project, and evidence that plugged and abandoned wells in the area will not have an adverse effect on the project or cause damage to life, health, property, or natural resources." The compilation's history note lists the September 3, 1996 change to subsections (a)(4) and (a)(5) as the last change (2011 p.110), so this requirement was in force for at least 19 years before the October 2015 notice. The offshore rule § 1748.2 had a similar item: "Casing diagrams, including cement plugs and cement fill behind casing, of all idle, plugged and abandoned, or deeper-zone producing wells within the area affected by the project" (2011 p.54).
- **Scope of the pre-2019 rule.** The old rule asked for diagrams only of idle, plugged and abandoned, or deeper-zone producing wells in the area affected by the project. It did not list the content each diagram had to show. The 15 items of § 1724.7.1(a) became mandatory on April 1, 2019 (2019c pp.10 and 11). A diagram submitted before 2019 that lacks those items should therefore not be described as violating the rules in force when it was submitted (inference).
- **District practice.** The June 2011 US EPA audit report (Horsley Witten Group, "California Class II Underground Injection Control Program Review", 490 pp, not saved, URL in section 5.2) records District 3 saying:
  - PDF p.109 (report p.89): "we did the reviews ourselves and provided the necessary information in table format. Today we require operators submit the information and require/accept only casing diagrams."
  - PDF p.120 (report p.100): "District 3 has historically conducted the area of reviews and maintained the data in tabular form. Currently, operators are required to submit updated AORs for new drills, conversions, and reactivation of all previously approved injection wells, including casing diagrams."
- **Staff effort in 2011.** District 2's questionnaire answer (PDF p.380) says an initial review "can take 2-3 hours because it would entail preparing a complete casing diagram from detailed analysis of all well histories", and later reviews 15 to 30 minutes. This is Division staff time, not operator time.
- **Inference.** The audit evidence suggests the work of assembling casing diagrams was shifting from district staff to operators before 2015, and the 2015 to 2019 actions made the content uniform and mandatory.

Points for the project:

- California is a Class II primacy state (1982 agreement, 2019b p.3), so the federal requirement is the floor its program operates under. The federal rule asks for "appropriate drawings" without a fixed list.
- California's 15-item list is more specific than the federal rule and the state examples above. Ohio's 10-item rule is the closest in form.
- Colorado and New Mexico content lists are agency guidance, not regulation text.
- The comparison is a sample of four states, not a survey.

### 3.3 Review cycle

§ 1724.6(d) (2019c p.5): "The Division will review existing underground injection projects periodically, but not less than once every three years". With 765 active projects that is at least 255 project reviews a year (inference). The cost analysis also assumes about 18 new projects a year in Years 2 to 5 (2018b p.23).

### 3.4 Idle wells

- 8,247 idle wells in 2018 had been idle 15 years or more and need the engineering analysis (2018a p.29).
- The cost analysis spreads the work over a four-year compliance period (about 2,062 wells a year, inference).
- Cost: $440 per well, or $280 to $600 per field or batch of wells, about 4 hours at $70 to $150 an hour (2018a p.29). This covers the whole engineering analysis, not only the casing diagram.
- 12 operators own 72% of idle wells (2018a p.8).

### 3.5 Gas storage

- 452 gas storage wells at 14 facilities (2016 pp.15 and 25).
- § 1726.4(a)(5)(F) requires graphical casing diagrams or flat file data sets for all wells in the area of review in the same or a deeper zone. § 1726.4.1(a)(1) lists 18 data elements, (A) to (R), and § 1726.4.1(b) allows a flat file.
- I found no statewide diagram count for gas storage.

### 3.6 One administrative action: Order 1517

Order to Perform Remedial Work and Pay Civil Penalties No. 1517, Sentinel Peak Resources California LLC, April 24, 2025 (2025b).

- **Request and count (complete packet, pp.18-24):** on October 1, 2024, CalGEM requested review and correction of **all wellbore diagrams for both UIC projects**, with an October 18 deadline (17 days). It identifies common problems in many diagrams and says required data were missing for each wellbore. The packet does not state the number of wells or diagrams; the two diagram PDFs named as examples in the email are not included.
- **Workload and extensions (pp.21, 24):** CalGEM extended the deadline to December 20. On December 18, the operator requested a further 12 weeks. It reported that by December 16 it had onboarded 6 full-time contract employees and 3 part-time contract employees and reassigned 3 of its own technicians. At that point, about 52% of East Project diagrams had been edited, and work on West Project diagrams had not begun. The operator said a significant number of wells remained and that the work included data validation and uploading diagrams to WellSTAR.
- **Outcome (pp.27-28):** the Notice of Violations states that the operator failed to submit the requested updates by December 20 and lists one violation for each project. This is not a diagram count.

The cost analysis assumes the project-by-project review is done "over the first two years" (2018b p.28). That is a costing assumption, not a deadline in the rule.

### 4.3 Assessment (inference)

- Operators had a long runway before the new rules (about 3.5 years for injection projects, about 16 months from the formal proposal for gas storage), and the Division's own discussion drafts and workshops spanned 2015 to 2018.
- The rules do not give a dated schedule for producing casing diagrams. Time pressure is event-driven: a Division request, a violation notice, well work, or an idle well milestone.
- In the one documented enforcement case (Order 1517), the operator had 17 days at first and received 80 days in total before a Notice of Violation. I did not find a public case in which a large batch of diagrams was due on a short deadline.
- The workload is concentrated. Roughly 10 operators own 92% of injection wells, so the statewide estimate of about 5,000 diagrams a year in the first two years falls mostly on them.
