Applied Concepts — INFQ 70303

From Janitorial Metrics to Value Framing

Study guide connecting John Talburt’s Principles of IQ lectures (Aug 25 & 27, 2026) to field practice. Anonymized examples from upstream oil & gas data work.

Core Lesson

What Talburt Wants You to Remember

Information quality is about creating value and minimizing risk — not celebrating data cleaning as the product.

  • Data quality (inward): Is the data right? Measured in %, completeness, conformance.
  • Information quality (outward): Are we building the right thing? Measured in money, risk, mission, quality of life.
  • Don’t confuse results with value. “Fixed 87% of errors” is a result. “Geologists resumed reservoir characterization without a three-month delay” is value.
  • Historically, the field started with janitorial cleansing (1980s warehousing). Mature IQ moves to product thinking, governance, and enterprise value.

Framework

The Value Chain (Use on Every Report)

  1. Customer — Who needs the information?
  2. Blocked — What decision or process was at risk?
  3. Delivered — What we did (keep brief).
  4. Enabled — What became possible? (This is the headline.)

Rule: Never stop at step 3 for management. Metrics like % fixed or wells loaded are footnotes.

Anonymized Examples

Janitorial vs. Value Framing

Wellbore path restoration (post-acquisition)

  • Janitorial: “Bot fixed 87% of errors on 14,700 wells in two weeks.”
  • Value: Compliance and geology teams needed wellbore path data for regulatory reporting and reservoir characterization. Acquisition left a large gap; manual work would have taken two analysts ~3 months. Automation restored ~12,800 wells in two weeks so characterization and compliance workflows continued without a multi-month delay.

Annual audit well counts

  • Janitorial: “Investigated 148 missing wells in an association table.”
  • Value: Internal audit team could not reconcile well counts for the annual audit. Restoring records unblocks official well-inventory validation — a risk-minimization story, not a row count.

GIS / spatial integrity

  • Janitorial: “Fixed field names and coordinates in the master system.”
  • Value: Operations reported wells missing from GIS and two wells plotted in the wrong state on spatial views. Correcting location integrity protects maps and decisions that depend on them.

BI platform migration (Tableau exit)

  • Janitorial: “Converted another dashboard to Spotfire.”
  • Value: Legacy BI platform ends December 31, 2026. Reservoir engineers lose constraint/surveillance views unless migrated before cutoff — business continuity, not IT plumbing.

Call Out Sheet validation app

  • Janitorial: “Built a GUI with six validation tests in PowerShell.”
  • Value: Prevent bad well-header instructions from reaching field contractors before they affect downstream systems. Technology is the footnote; wrong mobilization is the risk.

Practice

When Requests Lack Context

Customers often ask for tools (“convert this dashboard”) without stating impact. Complete the chain with three questions:

  1. What decision or workflow does this support? Who else uses it?
  2. Is there a deadline (audit, platform shutdown, planning cycle)?
  3. What breaks if this waits 30–60 days?
