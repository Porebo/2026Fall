Dispersion and Standard Scores

Range, variance, and standard deviation describe spread; variance is the square of standard deviation. For a population, the variance calculation divides the sum of squared deviations by N; a sample calculation uses n − 1. A z-score expresses a value's distance from the mean in standard deviations, positive above the mean and negative below it. (~05:00–15:45)

Counting Outcomes

For n distinct objects, the number of unordered pairs is n(n − 1)/2. More generally, combinations count unordered selections: choosing three people from ten produces 120 possible committees. For two fair dice, the 36 ordered outcomes give one way to total 2 and three ways to total 4. (~20:00–35:45)

Conditional Probability and Bayes

Conditional probability changes the denominator to the outcomes satisfying the given condition. Two card draws with replacement are independent: the chance of two aces is (4/52) × (4/52). Without replacement, the second probability depends on the first draw. (~45:00–50:45)

Bayes' theorem combines a prior probability with the likelihood of evidence. Do not confuse P(A given B) with P(B given A). The genetic-test example illustrates why a rare condition can remain unlikely even after a positive result: false positives among the much larger unaffected group matter. (~55:30–70:45)
