Surveys and Quality Dimensions

Assessment can combine subjective perspectives with objective measurements. Surveys can compare the views of collectors, custodians, and users; contrasting or repeated questions can help check consistency. Differences between groups may reveal where further investigation is needed. (~05:00, ~15:00)

The lecture distinguishes completeness from the amount of information and discusses interpretability, understandability, and consistent representation. The relevant dimensions depend on what consumers need from the information. (~10:00)

Objective Ratings and Timeliness

One completeness rating is 1 − (defective or missing values / total values assessed): zero defects yields 1, while all defective yields 0. Define both the defect and denominator before interpreting a percentage. (~20:00)

The timeliness example uses C for current age and V for useful lifetime. The linear score max(1 − C/V, 0) reaches zero when the useful lifetime is exceeded. Raising the score to a positive sensitivity exponent changes the curve; for a three-hour-old item with a four-hour lifetime, exponent 0.5 changes 0.25 to 0.5. (~25:00–30:45)

Combining and Displaying Ratings

Different ratings can be aggregated using a minimum when the weakest dimension is decisive, or a weighted sum when the weights reflect relative importance. For ratings and weights between 0 and 1, weights summing to 1 keep the weighted result within that range. A radar chart can display multiple dimensions together, though each axis still needs a defined interpretation. (~24:00, ~35:00–40:35)
