Sampling Error and Confidence

The distribution of sample means has standard error σ/√n when independent observations have population standard deviation σ. Increasing sample size reduces the standard error. The lecture discusses estimating the unknown population standard deviation with the sample standard deviation for sufficiently large samples. (~05:00–20:45)

A confidence interval combines a sample estimate and a margin of error. The 95% confidence interpretation refers to the method: across repeated samples, about 95% of intervals constructed this way would contain the fixed population parameter. A wider confidence level generally requires a wider interval when other inputs stay fixed. (~10:00–25:30)

Testing a Claim

Hypothesis testing compares a claim with data under a specified model and significance threshold. The lecture asks whether an observed sample result is sufficiently inconsistent with a proposed population value to reject that claim. A rejection is not proof of every alternative explanation, and the conclusion depends on the assumptions and chosen threshold. (~30:00–35:45)

Proportions

For a proportion p and sample size n, the standard-error expression discussed is √[p(1 − p)/n]. For a 95% normal interval, 1.96 standard errors is the usual two-sided multiplier; using 2 is a convenient approximation. (~40:00)
