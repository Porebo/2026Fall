Principles of IQ Vocabulary

An alphabetical dictionary based on the class notes. Use the letter index to find a term and its lecture sources.

Dictionary Index

A   B   C   D   E   F   G   H   I   L   M   N   O   P   Q   R   S   T   U   V   Z

A

Accessibility
Whether authorized users can obtain the information they need. (Lecture sources)
Accuracy
How closely a value corresponds to the true or accepted value; assessing it requires a trustworthy comparison, not merely a plausible format. (Lecture sources)
Anomaly
An unusual or unexpected observation. It becomes a quality defect when it violates a relevant requirement. (Lecture sources)
Appropriate amount
Whether the quantity of information is suitable for the task, rather than too little or too much. (Lecture sources)
Attribute chart
A control chart tracking counts or proportions, such as defective items. (Lecture sources)

B

Bayes' theorem
A rule for updating the probability of a hypothesis using prior probability and the likelihood of observed evidence. (Lecture sources)
Believability
The extent to which information is regarded as credible or believable. (Lecture sources)
Binomial distribution
A model for the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials with the same success probability. (Lecture sources)
Blank value
A value containing only whitespace, such as spaces or tabs. (Lecture sources)
Breadth of coverage
Whether the members of a target population are represented in the data. (Lecture sources)
Business glossary
A reference defining business terms, their meanings, and, where applicable, the rules used to derive them. (Lecture sources)

C

Central limit theorem
Under suitable conditions, the distribution of sample means approaches a normal distribution as sample size increases. (Lecture sources)
Cluster sampling
Selecting groups or clusters of population members for study. (Lecture sources)
Column analysis
Profiling a field's characteristics, such as value frequencies, missing values, patterns, and range. (Lecture sources)
Common-cause variation
Variation arising from the usual operation of a process. (Lecture sources)
Completeness
The extent to which required data is present. Coverage can concern both the population represented and the details available for each record. (Lecture sources)
Concise representation
Presenting information compactly without unnecessary redundancy or loss of needed meaning. (Lecture sources)
Conditional probability
The probability of an event given that another event has occurred. The given condition defines the relevant denominator. (Lecture sources)
Confidence interval
An interval calculated from a sample to estimate a population parameter. A 95% method captures the parameter in about 95% of repeated samples under its assumptions. (Lecture sources)
Control chart
A time-ordered process chart used to distinguish routine variation from signals that may warrant investigation. (Lecture sources)
Correlation
A measure of the direction and strength of association; Pearson's coefficient describes linear association on a scale from −1 to 1. (Lecture sources)
Critical data element (CDE)
A data item whose absence or poor quality could significantly affect a process or the organization. (Lecture sources)

D

Data catalog
An inventory describing available data, where it is located, and its characteristics. (Lecture sources)
Data dictionary
A reference describing data elements and their characteristics, such as names, definitions, and formats. (Lecture sources)
Data governance
The framework of roles, responsibilities, and rules for making decisions about organizational data. (Lecture sources)
Data lake
A repository that can retain data in its original form so it can be reprocessed as models and needs change. (Lecture sources)
Data management
The operational decisions and activities for storing, transforming, moving, and using data within governance rules. (Lecture sources)
Data portability
The ability to move and use data elsewhere with enough shared structure and meaning for the receiving system to interpret it. (Lecture sources)
Data profiling
Examining actual data to discover its values, patterns, relationships, and anomalies. Profiling alone does not determine whether an anomaly violates a requirement. (Lecture sources)
Data quality (DQ)
The degree to which data conforms to defined data requirements. A quality judgment depends on the requirements being applied. (Lecture sources)
Data quality assessment
Evaluation of data against requirements, informed by profiling the data and understanding the architecture or process that uses it. (Lecture sources)
Data requirement
A documented condition data must meet for its intended use, such as a required field, allowed code, or relationship. (Lecture sources)
Data stewardship
Care of data on behalf of the organization, including applying standards and coordinating with affected stakeholders. (Lecture sources)
Deduplication
Identifying and resolving records that represent the same entity, while taking care not to merge different entities. (Lecture sources)
Depth of coverage
Whether the required details for each represented member are present. (Lecture sources)
Determinant analysis
Checking whether one field or set of fields consistently determines another, such as whether an identifier maps to the expected value. (Lecture sources)

E

Empty value
A value containing no characters, such as a zero-length string. (Lecture sources)

F

Fitness for use
Whether a product meets the needs of its intended users and purpose. The same data can be useful for one product and unsuitable for another. (Lecture sources)
Foreign key
A field or set of fields that references a key in another table, linking related records. (Lecture sources)
Frequency distribution
A summary of how often values or value ranges occur. (Lecture sources)

G

Golden record
A consolidated or authoritative representation of an entity; modern master systems may also retain its history. (Lecture sources)
Governance charter
An executive mandate stating the governance program's purpose, value proposition, and scope. (Lecture sources)
Governance council
A cross-functional group that reviews data decisions and establishes or approves governance standards. (Lecture sources)

H

Histogram
A chart displaying frequencies across intervals. (Lecture sources)
Hypothesis test
A procedure for evaluating how consistent sample evidence is with a specified claim under a model and significance threshold. (Lecture sources)

I

Independence
Events are independent when knowing that one occurred does not change the probability of the other. (Lecture sources)
Information product
Data collected, processed, and presented for a particular use, such as a report, medical chart, or service. (Lecture sources)
Information quality (IQ)
The value and fitness of information for its intended use, including maximizing value and minimizing risk for the organization and its consumers. (Lecture sources)
Information quality gap
The disconnect between producing data that meets internal specifications and providing a product that actually meets consumer needs and creates value. (Lecture sources)
Interpretability
Whether the meaning of values, labels, units, and representations can be understood. (Lecture sources)

L

Linear regression
A method for fitting a line to describe or predict one variable from another. Its slope is not generally the correlation coefficient. (Lecture sources)

M

Manipulability
How readily information can be processed or adapted for the intended task. (Lecture sources)
Master data
Data describing entities important to an organization, such as customers, products, or employees. (Lecture sources)
Master data management (MDM)
Coordinating data about key entities, including identifiers and records used across systems. (Lecture sources)
Mean
The arithmetic average of a set of values. (Lecture sources)
Median
The middle value of an ordered set, or the average of the two middle values when the set has an even number of observations. (Lecture sources)
Metadata
Data describing other data, including its meaning, structure, format, origin, or rules. (Lecture sources)
Mode
The most frequently occurring value in a dataset. (Lecture sources)

N

Net present value (NPV)
The present value of future benefits and costs, discounted to account for when they occur. (Lecture sources)
Normal distribution
A symmetric continuous distribution described by its mean and standard deviation. Probabilities correspond to areas under its curve. (Lecture sources)
np chart
An attribute chart tracking the count of defective items. (Lecture sources)
Null
A database marker representing the absence of a known value. (Lecture sources)

O

Objective measure
A measure based on observable checks or calculations. (Lecture sources)
Objectivity
The extent to which information is unbiased and impartial. (Lecture sources)

P

p chart
An attribute chart tracking the proportion of defective items. (Lecture sources)
Parameter
A numerical characteristic of a population. (Lecture sources)
Pareto chart
A chart ordering categories by frequency, often with a cumulative line, to help prioritize investigation. The 80/20 pattern is a heuristic, not a guarantee. (Lecture sources)
Parsing
Separating a value into meaningful components, such as splitting a full name into fields. (Lecture sources)
Pattern profiling
Summarizing character patterns to identify variations in how values are formatted. (Lecture sources)
Placeholder
A substitute value such as “TBD” that may indicate missing information; its meaning must be interpreted in context. (Lecture sources)
Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA)
A continual-improvement cycle used in the course's ISO 8000-61 reference model: plan requirements, perform control, check assurance, and analyze and improve. (Lecture sources)
Population
The full group of interest in a study. (Lecture sources)
POSMAD
The information life-cycle activities: plan, obtain, store and share, maintain, apply, and dispose. Applying information is the stage in which it produces benefits. (Lecture sources)
Primary key
A field or combination of fields used to uniquely identify a row in a table. (Lecture sources)
Progress metric
A measure of work completed, such as standards approved or data items cataloged. (Lecture sources)

Q

Quality assurance (QA)
Checking inputs and processes against requirements needed to produce a conforming product. Good inputs alone do not guarantee a good output. (Lecture sources)
Quality control (QC)
Checking the finished information product against its requirements. (Lecture sources)
Quality rating
A numerical measure of a defined quality characteristic. A completeness rating may be 1 − (missing or defective values / values assessed), with a clearly defined numerator and denominator. (Lecture sources)

R

RACI
A role-assignment model: Responsible does the work; Accountable answers for it; Consulted provides input; Informed is notified. (Lecture sources)
Random variable
A numerical value assigned to an outcome of a random process, such as the sum of two dice. (Lecture sources)
Range
The difference between the maximum and minimum observed values. (Lecture sources)
Reference data
Codes and classifications used to represent shared meanings, such as country or currency codes. (Lecture sources)
Referential integrity
The requirement that applicable foreign-key values have matching referenced records. (Lecture sources)
Relevance
The extent to which information is applicable to the task or decision at hand. (Lecture sources)
Representational consistency
Expressing the same meaning consistently across records and systems. (Lecture sources)
Reputation
The regard in which the source or content is held by its users. (Lecture sources)
Return on investment (ROI)
Net benefit relative to the investment: (benefits − cost) / cost, often expressed as a percentage. (Lecture sources)
Root cause analysis
Investigating the underlying data or process conditions that produce an observed defect rather than addressing only the symptom. (Lecture sources)

S

Sample
A subset of the population selected for study. (Lecture sources)
Sampling distribution
The distribution of a statistic across possible samples drawn under the same sampling procedure. (Lecture sources)
Sampling error
The difference between a sample estimate and the population parameter it estimates. (Lecture sources)
Security
Protection of information from unauthorized access or disclosure; it must be balanced with appropriate access. (Lecture sources)
Semi-structured data
Data combining organized elements and free-form content, such as an email with structured headers and a free-text body. (Lecture sources)
Simple random sampling
Selecting population members randomly using a defined random-selection process. (Lecture sources)
Special-cause variation
Variation associated with a particular assignable cause outside ordinary process behavior. (Lecture sources)
Standard deviation
A measure of spread around the mean, equal to the square root of the variance. (Lecture sources)
Standard error
The standard deviation of a statistic's sampling distribution. For independent observations, the standard error of the mean is σ/√n. (Lecture sources)
Standardization
Transforming representations into an agreed consistent form while preserving their meaning, such as using an agreed date format. (Lecture sources)
Statistic
A quantity calculated from a sample, which can be used to estimate a population parameter. (Lecture sources)
Stratified sampling
Dividing the population into meaningful groups and sampling within those groups. (Lecture sources)
Structured data
Data organized in a predictable, machine-readable form, such as records in a relational table. (Lecture sources)
Subjective measure
A measure based on people's judgments, for example through surveys. (Lecture sources)
Systematic sampling
Selecting at a regular interval, such as every twentieth record. Patterns in ordering can bias the sample. (Lecture sources)

T

Timeliness
Whether information is sufficiently current and available when needed for its use. (Lecture sources)
Transactional data
Data recording events or activities, such as purchases, claims, or payments. (Lecture sources)

U

Understandability
How easily users can comprehend the information presented. (Lecture sources)
Unstructured data
Content such as narrative text that does not follow a fixed record structure for conventional processing. (Lecture sources)

V

Validation metric
A measure of whether the program achieves its intended value. (Lecture sources)
Validity
Conformance to rules such as allowed values, formats, and ranges. A valid value can still be inaccurate. (Lecture sources)
Value proposition
An explanation of the benefit an initiative or product is expected to create, such as reduced cost, improved health, or mission accomplishment. (Lecture sources)
Value-added
The extent to which information provides benefit or advantages to its users. (Lecture sources)
Variable chart
A control chart tracking measured quantities or statistics. (Lecture sources)
Variance
A measure of spread based on squared deviations from the mean. The usual sample variance uses n − 1 in its denominator. (Lecture sources)
Verification metric
A measure of whether people comply with established standards. (Lecture sources)

Z

z-score
A value's distance from the mean expressed in standard deviations. (Lecture sources)
