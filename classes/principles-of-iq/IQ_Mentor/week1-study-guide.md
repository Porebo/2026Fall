# Week 1 Study Guide: Principles of IQ (INFQ 70303)

## Deck A: IQ Graduate Program Overview (8/25)
- **UALR definition of IQ:** an Information Science discipline concerned with (1) maximizing the value and minimizing the risk of an organization's information assets, and (2) assuring that the information products the organization produces create value for the people and systems that use them.
- **Other definitions on the slide:** accuracy; fitness for use; accurate, complete, timely information free from defect; right information at the right time, place, and people; applying TQM principles to building information products.
- **Six domains of IQ (IQ International):** value & business impact; strategy & governance; environment & culture; measurement & improvement; sustaining IQ; information architecture quality.
- **Why study IQ:** poor IQ costs billions and lives. Generative AI models are only as good as their training data, and agentic AI can be used to improve IQ.
- **Program facts:**
  - MSIQ admissions opened May 10, 2006; first class was Fall 2006.
  - MSIQ is 33 hours. The graduate certificate (GC) is the first 12 hours: INFQ 7303, 7367, 7342, plus an INFQ elective.
  - The PhD is 75 hours: 33 lecture and 42 dissertation.
  - Standards named: ISO 8000 and ISO 25500.
  - Founding sponsors: Acxiom, the MIT IQ Program, and UALR.

## Deck C: IQ Principles and Domains (8/27)
- **History phases:**
  - 1980s data cleansing, when IQ was recognized as a problem.
  - Prevention, using TQM and root cause analysis.
  - Information product management (TDQM), the manufacturing analogy.
  - Quality by design, reflected in the information architecture.
  - Enterprise view: information as an organizational asset (data governance, data quality, MDM, analytics).
- **Emerging topics:** ISO 8000; streaming data; unstructured data; IoT; ML training-data bias and data poisoning; Data Washing Machines; data literacy, democratization, and monetization (infonomics).
- **Seven principles:**
  1. Data is a resource for producing information products (IPs) that create value. **VALUE = BENEFITS − INVESTMENT.** Don't confuse results with value.
  2. Control data as an organizational asset (data governance). Governance sets the rules for data management decisions. It is led by the Chief Data Officer (CDO).
  3. Information produces value only when it is used for a purpose.
  4. Manage information as a product, not a by-product. Product management means understanding consumer needs, managing a defined production process, managing the life cycle, and appointing an information product manager.
  5. IQ and DQ are related but different. DQ has an inward focus, is measured in percentages, and means building the thing right. IQ has an outward focus, is measured in value units, and means building the right thing.
  6. IQ is contextual, not absolute. The same data can have a fixed DQ but different IQ in different uses. Example: a name/address list has high value for coupon mailing and low value for phone lookup.
  7. Data has many dimensions. Wang-Strong categories: Intrinsic, Contextual, Representational, Accessibility.
