Field Notes — Anonymized

From Janitorial Metrics to Value Framing

Professional observations on how upstream data & analytics teams report work to management, grounded in John Talburt’s Principles of Information Quality (INFQ 70303, Fall 2026). All employer, person, and system identifiers are generalized.

Disclaimer: Examples are drawn from real work situations but anonymized for this academic site. No employer name, employee names, or confidential identifiers appear here. Patterns are described as industry observations, not criticism of individuals.

Why This Matters for Thesis Work

Technical research gaps in upstream data quality (streaming DQ, AI-ready data, emissions integration) are well documented in Research Gaps in Data Quality for Upstream Oil Businesses. A complementary gap is organizational: data quality functions often deliver real value but report it as janitorial activity. When leadership cannot see impact, competent teams lose funding — even while defects remain.

Potential thesis angle: How upstream operators can institutionalize value-based reporting for data quality and analytics work, especially during platform migrations, acquisitions, and audit cycles.

Course application (shorter study guide): Principles of IQ — Applied concepts

Talburt’s Frame (Summary)

From the Aug 25–27, 2026 Principles of IQ sessions with John Talburt:

  • IQ maximizes value and minimizes risk of information assets.
  • Data quality asks: Is the data right? Information quality asks: Are we building the right thing? (Tom Redman)
  • Field history: cleansing (1980s warehousing) → prevention → product management → enterprise governance → interoperability.
  • Results (%, counts, speed) ≠ value (decisions enabled, risk reduced, mission, quality of life).
CUSTOMER     Who needs the information?
BLOCKED      What decision, audit, or process was at risk?
DELIVERED    What we did (brief — load, bot, rules, conversion)
ENABLED      What became possible — headline for management
METRIC       87%, 14,700 wells — footnote only

Pattern: Why DQ Teams Get Cut

In a prior role, a data quality department was dismissed after a decade centered on janitorial cleansing. Business intelligence tools had surfaced defects; the team responded with profiling, rules, and remediation. Leadership eventually saw activity without a clear impact story and eliminated the function.

Lesson: Activity metrics protect team pride. Impact metrics protect the team’s relevance. Cleaning is necessary work; it must not be the story told to executives.

Case 1: Management Round Table (Activity Deck)

A monthly “workflow round table” deck for a data & analytics group illustrated the framing problem. Accomplishments were listed as tasks: loaded data to a subsurface platform, fixed connection issues, added DQ rules, batch-loaded completion records, updated column identifiers. A scorecard celebrated LOADED vs. data not received for new drills.

What management heard: the team was busy moving files.

What was missing: customers (geology, engineering, compliance), decisions at risk, and outcomes enabled.

Ironically, the work-request process asked submitters “What are you trying to accomplish?” but the team did not apply the same standard to its own reporting.

One stronger slide in the deck

A Call Out Sheet QC application is a strong example of when an app is the right delivery mode: repeatable pre-release validation with guardrails for multiple users. The deck still led with “improving data quality” and emphasized the scripting stack rather than the risk of wrong field mobilization — a reporting fix, not a reason to avoid building the app.

Case 2: Wellbore Path Automation (Acquisition)

Situation

Multiple teams use wellbore path data for reservoir characterization supporting historical production analysis, current operations, and future development planning. After a recent acquisition, a large share of acquired wellbores lacked complete path data.

Stakeholders

  • Regulatory compliance (subset of ~1,200 wells under audit/reporting focus)
  • Geologists (could not find path information when needed)

Delivery

  • ~12,800 of 14,700 wells corrected (~87%)
  • Manual estimate: two analysts × ~3 months
  • Automated bot: ~2 weeks
  • Remaining ~13% still in progress; highest-impact workflows already covered

Value framing (correct)

Unblocked reservoir characterization and compliance reporting during asset integration; avoided a multi-month delay in subsurface and regulatory workflows. The bot and the 87% are evidence, not the headline.

Case 3: Inbox Opportunities Not Reported Upward

Real customer pain often appears in email threads that never reach management slides.

Annual audit well counts

An internal audit group reported that ~148 wells were not appearing in a well–completion association table, blocking a query used to validate well counts for the annual audit.

  • Janitorial report: “Fixed 148 missing wells.”
  • Value report: Restored audit-ready well inventory for annual validation; reduced risk of incomplete or incorrect official counts.

GIS spatial integrity

Operations reported wells missing from GIS and wells plotted in the wrong state on spatial views (e.g., assets associated with one region appearing near another). Three wells never reached GIS; two had incorrect coordinates.

  • Janitorial report: “Updated coordinates in master system.”
  • Value report: Restored map integrity for operational and planning workflows; prevented decisions based on geographically wrong well locations.

BI conversion request without context

A reservoir engineer requested conversion of a “constraint oil and gross” surveillance view from a legacy BI tool to the corporate standard. The email named only the workbook — no deadline, no workflow, no impact.

With enterprise context (legacy BI platform ends December 31, 2026), the same request becomes: business continuity for diatomite reservoir surveillance before platform shutdown — not “another dashboard conversion.”

Team practice: When intake lacks context, ask three questions (workflow, deadline, pain if delayed) and record answers for round-table reporting.

Reporting Standards (Proposed)

Every management bullet uses four fields

  1. Customer / team
  2. What was blocked
  3. What we delivered (short)
  4. What was enabled (headline)

Ban as headlines (sub-bullets only)

  • “Improved data quality”
  • “Fixed errors” / “loaded X to Y”
  • “Added DQ rules” without stating what they protect
  • % complete without business context

Tableau exit program (through Dec 31, 2026)

Report conversions as a portfolio:

  • Critical views migrated / in progress / not started
  • Business owner and workflow per view
  • Days to platform blackout

Open one management slide each month with customer impact from the inbox, not only closed task logs.

Research Questions (Draft)
