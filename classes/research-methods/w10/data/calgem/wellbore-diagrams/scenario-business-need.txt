SCENARIO AND BUSINESS NEED
Validating an Existing Wellbore Diagram Before a Well Abandonment Filing
Version 1.0, November 11, 2026

Every statement of fact below is drawn from a source listed in Section 7. Where the
sources do not establish a point, this document says so.


1. PURPOSE
==========

This document describes a real, recurring task in California oil and gas
operations: checking whether a wellbore diagram already on file with the State
meets the State's content requirements before it is reused in a new filing. It
explains why the task exists, why it is done by a person today, and why it cannot
simply be skipped. It is the business context for the study of whether an AI
assistant can perform a first-pass review of such diagrams.


2. THE SCENARIO
===============

Actor:     a regulatory or engineering staff member at an oil and gas operator.

Trigger:   the operator plans to plug and abandon a well and must file a Notice
           of Intention to Abandon (Form OG 108) with the California Geologic
           Energy Management Division (CalGEM) through WellSTAR, CalGEM's online
           system. The notice must be filed at least ten days before work begins,
           and the notice must include wellbore diagrams showing the well's
           current and proposed condition. [Source 4]

Starting   the worker retrieves the most recent wellbore diagram already
point:     submitted for the well. Diagrams are stored in WellSTAR as document
           attachments to individual well records; CalGEM publishes no dataset of
           their contents. [Sources 5, 8]

Task:      before relying on that diagram, the worker confirms that it contains
           every item of data the State requires of a casing diagram, as set out
           in Title 14, California Code of Regulations, section 1726.4.1.
           [Source 1]

Output:    a determination, item by item, of which required data the diagram
           contains and which it lacks, so that missing data can be found and the
           diagram corrected before filing.

Standard:  section 1726.4.1, reproduced verbatim in casing-diagram-specification.txt.
           Section 1726.4.1 governs underground gas storage wells. Substantially
           similar casing diagram requirements apply to injection project wells
           (section 1724.7.1) and idle wells (section 1772.1.3). [Source 8]
           Section 1726.4.1 is used here as the most recently reaffirmed statement
           of what a complete casing diagram contains. [Source 2]


3. WHY THE DIAGRAM MUST BE VALIDATED
====================================

3.1 The State requires diagrams with abandonment notices.
    The State's own abandonment contract template requires that "The Notice of
    Intention to Abandon shall include wellbore diagrams of all the wells
    detailing their current and proposed condition," and lists seventeen items
    those diagrams must contain. [Source 4] CalGEM's guidance for reviewing
    notices in WellSTAR lists "Wellbore Diagram (current and proposed)" among the
    documents attached to a notice. [Source 5]

3.2 The State has found submitted diagrams to be inconsistent.
    In December 2025 CalGEM notified gas storage operators that it "has observed
    inconsistencies in the submission of updated casing diagrams," and directed
    that "all casing diagrams are updated and reflect the current condition of
    each well," within 60 days after well work. [Source 2]

3.3 The State reviews diagram content closely.
    CalGEM district engineers conduct an early review of area-of-review wellbore
    diagrams, "focusing on cement calculation methodology and well construction
    accuracy," to give operators earlier feedback on "time-consuming updates."
    [Source 3]

3.4 The State can order an operator to produce an accurate diagram.
    In an order to plug and abandon a well, CalGEM required the operator "to
    furnish a wellbore diagram accurately depicting the information included on
    the Well Summary." [Source 6]

3.5 An old diagram may not describe the well as it is now.
    A diagram on file reflects the well when it was drawn. Section 1726.4.1
    requires data such as the location of packers and other equipment, casing
    damage, and cement plugs with the dates they were placed [Source 1], all of
    which change with later well work. A diagram that was complete when filed can
    therefore be incomplete or out of date when reused.

3.6 Abandonment decisions depend on the diagram's content.
    The data the State requires include the base of protected groundwater, the
    tops and bottoms of cement behind casing and of cement plugs, perforation
    depths, and casing damage [Source 1]. These are the facts needed to decide
    where plugs must be placed. A missing item is not a formatting problem; it is
    a gap in the information the abandonment plan rests on.


4. WHY A PERSON DOES THIS WORK TODAY
====================================

4.1 The diagrams are documents, not data.
    Diagrams are filed as PDF drawings attached to well records [Source 8]. The
    regulation permits a flat file data set in place of a drawing [Source 1,
    subdivision (b)], but the diagrams on file are drawings. No field in a
    database can be queried to answer "does this diagram show the spud date."
    Someone has to read the drawing.

4.2 Diagrams follow no common layout.
    A text survey of the fourteen diagrams collected for this study, made on
    November 11, 2026 [Source 9], found:
      - years cited in the diagrams range from 1951 to 2022;
      - the amount of text on a diagram ranges from 755 to 9,324 characters;
      - protected groundwater is labeled "BFW" on some diagrams, "USDW" on
        others, and both on some;
      - measured depth is marked on 6 of the 14, true vertical depth on 1, and
        a spud date on 5.
    The same required data appears under different labels, in different
    places, and at very different levels of detail. A check must work across
    all of these layouts.

4.3 The requirement is written in legal language that calls for judgment.
    Several requirements bundle multiple facts into one item; for example, item
    (G) covers the sizes, weights, grades, and connection types of both casing
    and tubing [Source 1]. Others apply only in some wells, such as data for
    sidetracked well bores (item (Q)) or directional surveys (paragraph (3)).
    Deciding whether an item is satisfied, partly satisfied, or does not apply
    requires reading the diagram against the rule, item by item.

4.4 The operator, not a tool, answers to the State.
    The notice is the operator's filing. The State reviews the content
    [Source 3], has found inconsistencies [Source 2], and can order corrections
    [Source 6]. Responsibility for a deficient filing rests with the operator and
    the people who prepare it. No tool supplied by the State performs this check.

4.5 Errors are costly in time.
    A notice must be on file before work begins [Source 4]. A deficient diagram
    found by the State during review must be corrected and resubmitted, and
    CalGEM itself describes such updates as "time-consuming" [Source 3]. A
    deficiency found by the operator before filing costs less than one found by
    the State after.

4.6 The volume is large and will continue.
    CalGEM's 2021 well records list 67,693 active and 40,259 idle wells,
    107,952 unplugged wells in all, among 197,169 distinct wells [Source 7].
    Each unplugged well will eventually require an abandonment notice with
    wellbore diagrams. The check described in Section 2 recurs for each one.


5. CONCLUSION: THE CHECK CANNOT BE SKIPPED
==========================================

The abandonment notice requires wellbore diagrams [Sources 4, 5]. The State has
found that submitted diagrams are inconsistent [Source 2], reviews their content
[Source 3], and can order an operator to correct them [Source 6]. The diagrams on
file are drawings in no common layout [Sources 8, 9], measured against a requirement
written in legal language that calls for item-by-item judgment [Source 1].

For these reasons the validation is performed today by a qualified person, who
reads each diagram against each requirement and remains accountable for the
result. That person cannot be removed from the task. The question for this study
is narrower: whether an AI assistant can perform a reliable first-pass review
that the person then verifies, reducing the time spent on each diagram without
reducing the care taken over it.


6. WHAT THE SOURCES DO NOT ESTABLISH
====================================

- No source reviewed states that a wellbore diagram must accompany every type of
  Notice of Intention. The sources establish it for abandonment notices
  [Source 4] and list current and proposed diagrams among notice documents
  generally [Source 5].
- No source reviewed gives the time a person spends checking one diagram, the
  rate at which submitted diagrams are found deficient, or the cost of a
  rejected filing. These would have to be measured or obtained from operators.
- The seventeen items in the abandonment contract template [Source 4] and the
  eighteen items in section 1726.4.1 [Source 1] overlap but are not identical.
  This study uses section 1726.4.1 as its standard.
