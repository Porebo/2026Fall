Learning Module

Week 7 Analyzing and Interpreting Your Results

Once you have collected your data (evidence) for your research objectives, you need to correctly analyze and interpret your results. This week students will review basic analytical techniques and formulate a plan for continuing learning and improvement of their analytical skills.

Class Materials

Week 7 Class Materials

Here you will find any handouts and presentation materials for this week's unit.

  • What Questions are you seeking to Answer?
    These are not the only questions you can ask of your data, but this is a good starting point. Other types of questions may involve determining the optimal solution for a set of conditions (also known as prescriptive analysis). Note: Use the page down key to progress from slide to slide for this online document.
  • What Types of Data do you need to Answer your Questions?
    There are other ways of describing your data. For example, another way to look at your data is whether you are looking for structured data or non-structured (NOSQL) data like text.
  • How to Choose the Right Exploratory Analysis for your Data?
    This article is a nice start to understanding exploratory data analysis techniques. You can also check out references like Answering Questions with Data (https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/737) to further build your data summary skills.
  • How to Choose the Right Machine Learning Technique for your Data?
    This article provides a nice overview of Predictive/Machine Learning Techniques for consideration. Remember this article is a start, not a definitive list of all available techniques.
  • How to Test Statistical Assumptions about your Data?
    Every statistical, data mining, or machine learning techniques has certain assumptions about the data the technique was designed to work on. Think of it like a car. Each car has an assumption about the type of fuel it runs best on. If you have a gas powered vehicle, your fuel needs to be an acceptable type of gas (e.g., not diesel) for your engine to have the desired level of performance. As you review the different techniques available to you, be sure to read up on that technique to understand its strengths and limitations.
  • Overview of Text Mining
    For those students working with text in their research.
  • Overview of Image Analysis
    For those students working with images in their research.
  • Example of Specialty Analytics - Overview of Bioinformatics
    For those students interested in bioinformatics. Note: There are many types of specialty areas in analytics, including multimedia analytics, GIS analytics, signal/sound analytics, odor/chemical analytics, and the list goes on and on. The key lesson from all of this: Think about what data you can collect and what analytics are available to you for answering questions about your data.
