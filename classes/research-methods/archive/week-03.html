Week 3 Defining Variables and Hypotheses

This week we will look at the importance of systematically identifying your variables ( e.g., based on the data that you want to collect) and defining your research hypotheses, dependent variables or performance targets so you can assess how successful you are in achieving your research outcomes.

Instructions

What can you do with a literature review? The web has many articles on the steps involved with conducting a literature review (ex. https://www.scribbr.com/methodology/literature-review/). In addition, analyzing the literature review gives you a wealth of information regarding your research area, including what people are doing, how they are doing it, where they are doing it, when they are doing it, and why they are doing it. For this assignment, you will analyze a 2024 Journal Article that examines the Literature on "How Educators are Teaching AI in K-12 Education".


  1. How many articles did the authors review for this paper?
  2. What corpus (collections, sources, conference proceedings of papers) did they examine?
  3. What keywords did they use to find potential articles to include in their literature review?
  4. Describe how the authors organized and presented their findings from their Literature Review.
  5. What do you feel were the major findings and opportunities for further research that the authors discovered from their literature review?

Note: Check out the References. Notice that each reference listed can be matched to its usage in the paper. Also, notice that the author is using a style very similar to IEEE (numbered brackets) with the reference list organized alphabetically (some styles list the references in the order that they are mentioned in the paper).  In this course, you can use any of the major reference styles (APA, MLA, IEEE, etc.) - the important thing is to pick a style and apply it consistently whenever you reference/cite your literature sources.
