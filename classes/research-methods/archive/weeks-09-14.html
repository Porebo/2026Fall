Instructions

There is an old saying that goes "If you fail to plan then you need to plan to fail". This is very true for research projects. There is a lot to keep track of during a research project and lots of different milestones that you need to accomplish. This assignment is all about project management for research and you taking the first steps to report your progress to date to Dr. Wu and Dr. Dagtas.


For Tips on Project Management, check out these resources.


  1. Review the information/activities needed to complete the research project proposal. There are a number of resources on the web and you can find a nice overview at https://www.scribbr.com/research-process/research-proposal/.
  2. Review the most commonly used project management tools from https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/project-management-techniques-for-every-pm.
  3. Consider putting together a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS - https://www.projectmanagementdocs.com/template/project-planning/work-breakdown-structure/) to capture the different phases and activities you will need to accomplish to complete your own research proposal by the end of the semester.

Every student will need to prepare a 20 minute progress presentation (7 to 10 slides) to share with the class and submit them to the instructors. Students can voluntarily deliver their presentations in one of the scheduled sessions and receive feedback. If you are unable to attend any of the sessions, please submit your slides.


Presentations will start November 9 and continue November 16 and 23 as needed.  Students will be assigned the date to give their presentations.


Here are the items you will need to cover:

  • Title Slide for the Research Proposal - Include your Name, Title, Date
  • Slide to summarize the problem/challenge that you are going to address.  What are the research questions you plan to focus on.
  • Slide that outlines your research objectives.
  • Literature Review Slide(s) - What is your progress?  What search terms are you using?  What types of information are you looking for? What collections have you been searching?  What are some examples of the best articles you have found so far.  (Google Folder with Articles Collected to Date)
  • Slide Describing Your Research Methods for Collecting and Analyzing Your Data
  • Slide summarizing the contributions (merits) of this research.  Ex. Who would benefit? Who would find this research useful?
  • Slide Summarizing the Remaining Work that Needs to be Done to complete the Research Proposal.

Points will be awarded once you have completed the assignment in class.

Instructions

This is the culminating assignment for INFQ 72203 IQ Theory. Be sure to review how to write a research proposal from https://www.scribbr.com/research-process/research-proposal/. At a minimum, your proposal should include.

  1. Title Page - See https://www.papertrue.com/blog/dissertation-title-page/ for examples
  2. Table of Contents (Optional)
  3. Introduction- See Scribbr (link is located above) for more information
  4. Literature Review (aim for a minimum of 20 articles cited in the body of the paper and the majority of those articles should be peer reviewed).  Remember references reflect the papers that you have read and that you feel have something important to support your research proposal's case (e.g., introduction, how your work compares to others, methods used, novelty of work, contributions, etc.).  Consequently, you need to plan on examining 40 to 60 articles in order to identify the 20 strongest works to include in your literature review.
  5. Explanation of the Data You Plan to Collect, Your Research Design and Tentative Research Schedule.  Be sure to identify any limitations or risks that you anticipate and how you will handle those contingencies.
  6. Closing Remarks.  Be sure to explain the Contributions of Your Research to the Field and how you plan to communicate your research findings to others.
  7. Reference List (Remember to use a reference/citation style like APA, MLA, IEEE, etc. and apply it consistently).  For a reference to count, it must be cited in the text and referenced and contain a link to the article itself to validate the reference.  In addition, make sure you have full copies of all your references in your Google Folder that you have shared with Dr. Pierce and Dr. Wu (see the section below for more details on this).
  8. Acknowledgement of any AI Tools that you used to help brainstorm ideas, generated sources for the literature review, or other content that you used as part of the introduction, research design, contributions. Also, it is fine to acknowledge what AI Tools that you used to format, edit, and improve your writing.

Note: Assuming single space, 10 to 12 point font, your research proposal body should be around 8 to 10 pages.


In addition to your research proposal, you also need to include a link to a google drive folder for both Dr. Wu and Dr. Dagtas so we can verify your literature sources that you cited and referenced in your research proposal.

  • Include a full pdf copy of all articles.
  • Be sure to annotate, highlight, or mark up the sections of the article that you used as the basis of your reference.
  • If referencing a book, you need to provide a link to the full pdf text online (or other proof that you had access to reading the book or relevant chapters).
  • Make sure that each article is cited in your paper.  Consider using APA format (Author Name, Date) to make it easy to align your in-text citations with your article identifiers.
  • Make sure that your reference list has a complete set of information for the article (i.e., correct authors, title, publication source, date, etc.).  Again, use a format like APA to fully document your sources.

Check out https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/how-to-write-a-great-research-proposal/ for additional guidance.
